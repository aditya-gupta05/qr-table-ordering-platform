# Lantern Kitchen: Dish Guide

A guide to how each dish on the menu is prepared, what to expect and what the kitchen can change. Prices and allergen lists are in the ordering menu, which is the authority; this guide describes cooking and serving only. Where a dish can be altered, the chef decides on the day.

## Starters

### Chicken Satay

Thigh meat is marinated overnight in turmeric, lemongrass and coconut milk, then grilled over charcoal until the edges blister. The peanut sauce is made in small batches each morning and served warm in its own pot, with pickled cucumber on the side to cut the richness. Ask for the sauce on the side if you want to control how much you use. It is a gentle one-chilli dish. Pairs well with the Steamed Jasmine Rice.

### Crispy Calamari

Squid is cut into rings, dusted in seasoned flour and fried to order for about ninety seconds, then served with a lemon wedge and a lemon aioli made in-house each day. The batter is light, so the calamari should be eaten straight away. It shares a fryer with Fish and Chips and Onion Rings. The aioli can be served on the side, and the kitchen can leave out the lemon zest garnish if you prefer.

### Burrata & Heirloom Tomato

A whole ball of burrata is torn at the table-side pass over sliced heirloom tomatoes in a mix of colours, then finished with basil-pine nut pesto, a slow-reduced balsamic and flaky salt. Tomatoes vary with the season, so the colours and sweetness change from week to week. It is served cool, not cold, to bring out the flavour. The pesto can be served on the side if you want to taste the cheese alone.

### Edamame

Soybeans in the pod are steamed for four minutes, then tossed with flaky sea salt and chilli flakes. It is a simple, quick dish that arrives within minutes, which makes it a good first order for a hungry table. The chilli is mild; if you want it plain, ask for the salt only. Guests squeeze the beans from the pod with their teeth and leave the pods in the small bowl we provide.

### Pork Belly Bao

Pork belly is cured, braised for four hours in soy and star anise, glazed under the grill and tucked into two soft steamed buns with hoisin and pickled mustard greens. The buns are steamed to order, so they are pillowy rather than chewy. A portion is two buns, which suits sharing. Pork is cooked on its own flat-top and with its own tools. It is not a halal dish.

### Roasted Cauliflower Hummus

Chickpeas are blended until whipped and pale, then topped with cauliflower roasted until its edges are nutty, plus za'atar and olive oil. It is served with gluten-free crackers, which are kept in a sealed container, but the kitchen can swap them for warm flatbread if you prefer, which then contains gluten. Good for sharing among three. The tahini gives the hummus its depth, and the cauliflower is roasted fresh each service.

### Tuna Tartare

Sushi-grade tuna is diced by hand, folded with avocado, sesame and ponzu, and served in a ring with wonton crisps for scooping. It is the one raw dish on the menu and it is made to order, so it can take a few minutes longer than other starters. The portion is small and rich, and is best before a lighter main. Raw fish carries its usual risks for pregnant guests and people with weakened immunity.

### Tomato Basil Soup

Tomatoes are slow-roasted with garlic and thyme for two hours, blended with basil and finished with cream. The soup arrives hot and is smooth rather than chunky. It is the starter we most often recommend for a cold day or for a child's first course, since it is mild. A swirl of olive oil is added at the pass, and the kitchen is happy to leave it off.

### Satay Tofu Skewers

Firm tofu is pressed, marinated in turmeric and lemongrass, and grilled on its own skewers until it is golden at the edges. It is served with the same warm peanut sauce and pickled cucumber as the Chicken Satay. It was added as the plant-based alternative to the chicken skewers and is made on a cleaned section of the grill. A good pick if you want satay flavours without meat.

### Butternut Squash Soup

Butternut squash is roasted until caramelised, simmered with stock and coconut cream and blended until velvety. The garnish is toasted pumpkin seeds and a thin drizzle of chilli oil, which gives the soup a little warmth. It is a hearty, sweet soup, and it is the one we recommend to guests who want a warming starter without cheese or cream. The chilli oil can be left off for a milder bowl.

## Bowls & Curries

### Thai Green Curry

The paste is made in-house from green chillies, lemongrass, galangal and coriander root, then fried in coconut cream until the oil splits. Seasonal vegetables and Thai basil are added at the end so they stay crisp, and it is served over jasmine rice. It is the hottest dish on the menu, at spice level three. Fish sauce and shrimp paste are used in the base. Extra rice or a Mango Lassi calms the heat.

### Vegan Buddha Bowl

Brown rice is topped with roasted sweet potato, edamame, sliced avocado and pickled cabbage, with a tahini-lime dressing. The ingredients are arranged in sections so you can see each element. The dressing is poured over at the table. It is a filling, cool-temperature bowl that suits lunch, and each element is prepared separately, so the kitchen can leave out any item you dislike.

### Short Rib Rice Bowl

Beef short rib is braised for six hours in a gochujang and soy glaze until it falls apart, then served on steamed rice with kimchi and a fried egg with a runny yolk. It is one of our richest dishes and one of the largest. The glaze has a moderate heat, level two. The egg can be cooked through if you prefer it. The kimchi on the bowl is our house kimchi.

### Spicy Tofu Stir-Fry

Cubes of firm tofu are fried until crisp, then tossed in a very hot wok with Szechuan peppercorns, dried chilli and peppers. The peppercorns give a tingling numbness along with the heat, and it is served over rice. At spice level three it is as hot as the Thai Green Curry. If you are new to Szechuan peppercorn, ask for it to be reduced. The wok is shared, so tell the server about any allergies.

### Chicken Tikka Masala

Chicken is marinated in yoghurt and spices, charred in the wood-fired oven and simmered in a creamy tomato masala that is finished with a little ground almond. It is served with basmati rice. The sauce is medium spicy, at level two, and rich rather than hot. It is a reliable choice for people who want something familiar, and the sauce is a good match for flatbread ordered from your server.

### Mushroom Risotto

Arborio rice is toasted, then cooked slowly with celery stock, adding the liquid a ladle at a time for about eighteen minutes. Wild mushrooms are sauteed separately and folded through with parmesan and butter at the end. It is made to order, so expect about twenty minutes. The texture should be loose and creamy, not stiff. A half portion is available for children.

### Pad Thai

Rice noodles are stir-fried in a very hot wok with prawns, egg, bean sprouts, tamarind and fish sauce, then topped with crushed roasted peanuts and a lime wedge. It is sweet, sour and savoury, with a mild heat at level one. Squeeze the lime over it at the table. Because of the wok, the peanuts and the fish sauce, it is not a dish we can adapt for a peanut, fish or shellfish allergy.

## Mains

### Margherita Pizza

The dough is fermented for forty-eight hours, stretched by hand and baked for about ninety seconds in the wood-fired oven at around four hundred and fifty degrees. San Marzano tomato sauce, fior di latte mozzarella and fresh basil go on top. The crust blisters and chars in places, which is intended. It is the dish most often ordered as a half portion for children.

### Pesto Gnocchi

Potato gnocchi are rolled by hand each morning and boiled for two minutes to order, then tossed in basil and pine-nut pesto with parmesan and blistered cherry tomatoes. The sauce is bright green and fresh, not heavy. Because the pesto is made with pine nuts, it is one of the dishes the kitchen will not alter for a nut allergy. The parmesan is made with vegetarian-friendly rennet.

### Miso Glazed Salmon

A thick salmon fillet is marinated for a day in white miso, mirin and soy, then roasted until the glaze caramelises and the centre is just cooked. It is served on bok choy with sesame rice. We cook it medium by default, so tell your server if you prefer it well done. It is the most expensive main on the menu, and the portion is generous.

### Lemon Herb Roast Chicken

Half a free-range chicken is brined overnight, roasted in the wood-fired oven with lemon and thyme, and served with roast potatoes, charred lemon and a bright salsa verde. The skin is crisp and the meat is juicy. The salsa verde includes mustard, capers and herbs, and it can be served on the side. It takes around twenty-five minutes to come out, so order early.

### Fish and Chips

Cod fillet is dipped in a beer batter and fried in the shared fryer until crisp, then served with thick-cut chips, mushy peas and house tartare sauce. The batter is made fresh every few hours so it stays light. The chips here are the thick-cut potato chips, not our Shoestring Fries. It is a hearty main, and the tartare can be left off the plate.

### Lamb Kofta Plate

Minced lamb is spiced with cumin, coriander and a little cinnamon, shaped around skewers and grilled over charcoal. It is served with saffron rice, cucumber yoghurt and warm flatbread. The heat is gentle, at level one. The lamb comes from a halal-certified supplier. The flatbread is cooked in the wood-fired oven and can be left off the plate if you want the dish without bread.

### Beef Burger

A smashed beef patty is seared on the flat-top, topped with melted cheddar, pickles and house sauce, and served in a toasted brioche bun. It is served medium-well because the patty is thin and cooked through. The beef is not halal-certified. A burger is a simple option for guests who want something familiar, and the kitchen can leave out the pickles or the sauce.

## Sides

### Shoestring Fries

Potatoes are cut thin, rinsed, blanched and fried to a pale gold in the dedicated gluten-free fryer, then salted with sea salt. They are the only item cooked in that fryer. They are served in a paper cone and are best eaten while hot. A half portion is available for children. They pair well with Beef Burger or Lemon Herb Roast Chicken.

### Onion Rings

Thick slices of sweet onion are dipped in beer batter and fried in the shared fryer until deep golden, then served with a small pot of house sauce. They share oil with the Crispy Calamari and the Fish and Chips, so the fryer oil is mixed. They are best shared as a table. The batter is heavy compared with the calamari, so they stay crunchy for several minutes.

### Garlic Greens

Broccolini and green beans are flash-cooked in a hot wok with a lot of garlic and chilli oil, then finished with a squeeze of lemon. It is a quick side, arriving in about six minutes, with gentle heat at level one. The wok is shared with other dishes, so tell your server if you have an allergy. It balances richer main courses like the Short Rib Rice Bowl.

### Steamed Jasmine Rice

Jasmine rice is rinsed, steamed in small batches and fluffed just before it leaves the kitchen. It is a plain side that goes with curries, stir-fries and satay. A half portion is available for children. If you finish your curry and still have sauce left, ask for extra rice; it is the best value item on the menu.

### House Kimchi

Napa cabbage is salted, rinsed and fermented in-house for about a week with chilli, garlic, ginger and fish sauce. It is crunchy, sour and spicy, at level two, and served cold in a small bowl. Kimchi gets stronger the longer it ferments, so the batch we serve changes through the month. It is the same kimchi served with the Short Rib Rice Bowl.

## Desserts

### Sticky Toffee Pudding

A warm date sponge is baked in individual moulds, turned out and drenched in butterscotch sauce, with a scoop of vanilla ice cream on the side. The sponge is dense and moist. It is cooked to order, so it takes about eight minutes. It is the most popular dessert and is large enough to share between two.

### Flourless Chocolate Torte

Dark chocolate and butter are whisked with eggs and almond flour and baked low and slow, then chilled so the texture is fudge-like. It is served with raspberry coulis. Because the recipe uses almond flour and not wheat flour, it suits some gluten-free diets, but it contains almonds. The torte is made fresh daily and is served cold.

### Mango Sorbet

Two scoops of sorbet are made from ripe Alphonso mangoes, sugar and a little lime juice, churned in-house. It is fruity, bright and light, and has no dairy. It is the dessert we recommend after a spicy meal. A half portion is available for children. The sorbet is made in small batches and sometimes sells out on busy nights.

### Matcha Panna Cotta

Cream is set with agar and flavoured with ceremonial-grade matcha, then turned out and finished with black sesame crumble. It is set for at least six hours, so it is always ready. The matcha is slightly bitter, balanced by the sweet cream and nutty crumble. Because it is made with cream it is not vegan, and because of the black sesame crumble it is not suitable for sesame allergies.

### Coconut Rice Pudding

Short-grain rice is simmered slowly in coconut milk with sugar until creamy, then topped with toasted coconut flakes and a little lime zest. It is served warm. It is a gentle, comforting dessert that works for guests who avoid dairy, since coconut milk replaces cream. It is one of the few desserts that is entirely plant-based.

### Pistachio Kulfi

Milk is reduced slowly with cardamom, mixed with ground pistachio and frozen in a mould, which gives kulfi its dense texture compared with regular ice cream. It is served in a slice over a rose syrup. It is richer and slower to melt than ice cream. Pistachios are a tree nut, but there are no peanuts in the recipe.

### Spiced Apple Crumble

Apples are peeled, tossed with cinnamon, cloves and a pinch of nutmeg and baked under an oat crumble until golden. It is served with warm custard. Nutmeg is a spice from a seed and is not a tree nut. The crumble is baked to order, which takes about ten minutes. It is a warm, familiar finish for a cold evening.

## Drinks

### Fresh Lemonade

Lemons are squeezed to order and combined with sugar syrup, still water and a handful of bruised mint. It is tart rather than sweet. Refills are not free, but you can ask for a jug to share. It cleanses the palate between spicy dishes and is a good choice for children.

### Mango Lassi

Yoghurt, ripe mango pulp and a pinch of cardamom are blended with ice until smooth. It is thick, cooling and mildly sweet. It is the drink we recommend with the hottest dishes, such as the Thai Green Curry, since the dairy fat calms the heat. It is served in a tall glass with a straw.

### Oat Milk Latte

A double espresso is combined with steamed oat milk. We use oat milk for this drink because it steams well and it has a gentle sweetness. Please note that the oat milk contains gluten. We do not offer other plant milks. The latte is served hot in a ceramic cup.

### Iced Jasmine Tea

Jasmine green tea leaves are cold-brewed for twelve hours and served over ice, unsweetened. It is light and floral. The tea is brewed in batches each morning, so it is always fresh. It goes with almost every dish on the menu. Refills are available on request for a small charge.

### Ginger Beer

Fresh ginger is juiced, simmered with sugar and lime, then mixed with sparkling water to make a non-alcoholic house ginger beer. It has a real kick, at level one. It is a good alternative to a soft drink, and it is also a good mixer for guests who bring their own spirits to a private event.

### Pinot Grigio (glass)

A dry Italian white wine served by the glass, chilled to about eight degrees. It is the only wine we pour, chosen because it is crisp and goes with seafood, salads and lighter mains. A glass is a standard 150 ml pour. Wine contains sulphites. We do not sell the wine by the bottle, but guests may bring their own bottles for a corkage fee.
